# Hit and scene caller composition

The original source equations retain their contracts in
[MAIN-LOOP-LIFT.md](MAIN-LOOP-LIFT.md) and
[GLOVE-MOVEMENT-COMPOSITION.md](GLOVE-MOVEMENT-COMPOSITION.md).
Independent former callers in `proof/caller/` retain the original comparisons.
New integration comparisons use actual callees at budgets one and seven.
Upload completion remains a serviced boundary, separate from device and scene
oracle evidence. Hardware, sound, save layout and lag-free frame policy retain
their existing contracts.

## Block 1: hit confirmation

Both harmless-hit notices compose the real confirmation loop through their
original 80:87B6 and 80:87FC JSR frames. Real caller cases retain the complete
window and glyph uploads, thirty counter waits and karma boundaries. Delayed
confirmation cases reject directional input and accept each of B, Y, Select,
A and X. The retained former caller independently dispatches confirmation.

## Block 2: hit notice release

Both notices compose the actual release and window-list redraw through their
80:87B9 and 80:87FF JSRs. The hit service table drops confirmation and release.
Actual caller comparisons retain window allocation, last-index/tile restoration,
upload chronology and request wrap, with real glyphs at both budgets.

## Block 3: player-hit window list and entry

The player-hit path composes the real window list through 80:883B. The hit
entry receives only its optional text renderer, and main-upkeep removes the
remaining hit table. Actual callers compare player/nonplayer targets, damage
rejection and lethal/nonlethal records, free/full object lists and upload wrap
at both budgets. The sound command, eleven shake frames, hit effects, task
events and final game state are compared with the former hit caller using
the independently retained window-list dispatcher. The original random
per-function comparisons remain separate and unchanged.

## Block 4: explicit-weapon shot caller

The 80:8AB5 entry composes the actual hit through 80:89AC and accepts its
text renderer. Former shooting orchestration is retained in proof/caller,
including the independent random original comparisons. New real callers use
all shot kinds, warning/penalty notices, player hits, hit/miss, absent and
blocked targets, the ranged reach boundary and request wrap at both budgets.
The remaining two entries still dispatch their hits until their own blocks.

## Block 5: glove-target shot caller

The 80:8A51 entry composes its hit directly and receives the text renderer.
Caller comparisons now include both shot entries and every weapon kind zero
through seven, with the real weapon lookup, sounds and attacked flag for the
glove entry. Absent glove targets and its distinct blocked result are retained.

## Block 6: own-weapon shot caller

The 80:8A8C entry also composes its hit directly with only the optional text
renderer. All three shot entries use direct composition. The own-weapon
caller requires a target; the actual absent-target cases belong to the other
two entries. Its weapon, sight, turn, random, damage and full hit chain are
compared independently with the former caller at both budgets.

## Block 7: blast hits

The blast composes each reached hit through 80:8E8C, forwarding only its
renderer. Main-upkeep removes the shooting/blast hit table. The former blast
retains its independent original comparison with recorded hit arguments.
Actual callers compare both signs of the exclusive 60 reach on every axis,
warning/penalty transitions during multiple hits, player damage and sound,
effect allocation and the continuation to later slots after notices at both
budgets. An effect placed in an already scanned free slot is not hit again.

## Block 8: zone task creation

The first zone list calls the established task API directly below its saved
list offset and original 80:80A5 JSR frame. Main-upkeep drops the task service.
The former zones remain independently compared with the original instructions.
Actual callers append real script tasks on every inclusive coordinate boundary,
with zero, one and two overlapping zones, no player and full task slots, at both
budgets. Geometry and the explicit allocation failure retain their contracts.
